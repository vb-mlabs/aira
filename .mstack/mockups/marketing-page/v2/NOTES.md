# v2 — Hybrid magazine-leaning

**Hierarchy:** Bigger editorial commitment up top. Email capture is a deliberate mid-page conversion moment, not a hero element. Phone mockup is smaller and lower. Reads more like a thoughtful brand site, less like a SaaS landing page.

**Sections (in order):**

1. **Minimal nav** — logo + wordmark, single right-aligned "Get notified" link (text, not a button — quieter).
2. **Full-viewport editorial hero** — Cormorant headline takes most of the viewport, generous whitespace, italic tagline below, tree-of-life large and ornamental. No form here. Small "scroll" cue at the bottom.
3. **Manifesto long-form** — 2-3 paragraphs of editorial body copy explaining *why* AIRA exists — the curation philosophy, the community focus, the Nisarga mission. Lato body, Cormorant pull quotes.
4. **Pull quote** — full-width italic Cormorant quote with brass-gold accent line, attribution to the team.
5. **Categories editorial layout** — same 7 categories as v1, but presented in a more spaced, label-driven editorial layout (less grid, more list-with-color-bars).
6. **Phone mockup, smaller** — single phone-in-hand image (Home screen), captioned editorial-style with a one-liner.
7. **Email signup callout** — full-bleed cream-on-darker-cream callout box: "Be among the first 100 Atlanta neighbors to know when AIRA opens." Email input + button.
8. **Compact business panel** — 1 row, short copy, "Get in touch about being listed" mailto link (no separate form).
9. **Footer** — brand + tagline + contact + legal.

**Why it works:**
- The manifesto section makes the brand feel intentional, not just another directory app. That matters for trust with an older demographic and a culturally-specific audience.
- Mid-page email capture has higher intent quality (the visitor has now read the proposition).
- Less visual noise — more whitespace, slower rhythm.

**Tradeoffs:**
- Lower conversion velocity. Visitors who don't scroll past the manifesto won't see the email form.
- Risks feeling pretentious if the manifesto copy isn't tight.
- Phone mockup is smaller — less of a "see the product" moment.

**What I'd test post-launch if this is the winner:**
- Whether scroll depth predicts email signup quality (does the manifesto section actually filter?).
- Whether adding a small sticky "Get notified" prompt at scroll-30% helps without breaking the editorial tone.
